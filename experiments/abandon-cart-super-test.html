Hypothesis

H1: Cohort B (new series) will achieve a higher CVR than Cohort A (legacy series). H2: Cohort B will achieve higher engagement metrics (OR, CTR, CTO).
Target impact: 15%+ increase to CVR and engagement metrics (OR, CTR, CTO).

Experiment Details

Abandon-cart campaigns had gone stale — dated, text-based, repetitive messaging. Following the earlier cadence test and the rollout of the content flywheel, this Super Test resets the baseline entirely: a fully redesigned series goes head-to-head against the legacy series before returning to incremental A/B testing. What changed all at once: text-based templates → designed templates; 15-minute trigger → 8-minute trigger (closer to the 5.3-minute median time-to-convert); “Jamie” sender → “Solace Health” sender; 5 emails + 2 SMS → 4 emails + 2 SMS; simultaneous email/SMS sends → staggered sends; softer messaging → direct, CVR-focused messaging.

Impact summary

The redesigned Super Test series (Variant B) set a new performance baseline, with statistically significant improvements across every primary conversion metric — Started Eligibility Check (+23.9%), Completed Eligibility Check (+19.4%), and Physician Intake Booked (+23.4%) — all while sending one fewer email. Engagement also improved significantly (clicks +28.8%, CTO +27.6%) despite open rate staying essentially flat. Variant B becomes the new baseline going forward.

Learnings

What worked

  • The new series established a clear new baseline — Cohort B beat the legacy series on every primary conversion metric (Eligibility Check Starts +23.9%, Completions +19.4%, Physician Intake Bookings +23.4%), also validated directionally in Customer.io.
  • Modernized creative and more direct, CVR-focused messaging increased engagement — CTR and CTO both improved significantly even though opens stayed essentially flat, meaning the new content converted better once it was opened.
  • The redesigned journey did more with less — one fewer email, staggered sends, and still substantially higher downstream conversion.
  • Earlier trigger timing (8 min vs. 15 min, closer to the 5.3-minute median time-to-convert) is directionally promising, though it can't be isolated from the other simultaneous changes.
  • This compounds directly on the earlier Cadence & Timing win: Eligibility Check CVR climbed 2.93% → 3.07% → 4.49% across the original series, the 10-day cadence, and now this redesign.

What didn't

  • Open rate stayed largely unchanged — sender name, subject line, and visual redesign didn't meaningfully move opens, so future optimization should target click behavior and conversion rather than top-of-funnel engagement.
  • This was a Super Test, not a single-variable A/B test — timing, creative, messaging, sender, cadence, and sequencing all changed at once, so no individual change can be credited for the lift on its own.
  • Later touchpoints still show engagement decay — CTR and CTO decline across later emails, the same pattern seen before.

Follow-up actions

  1. Roll out the new series as the permanent baseline for the unverified abandon-cart journey.
  2. Shift from Super Tests back to incremental A/B tests to isolate individual variables: creative/design, messaging/value prop, trigger timing (8 vs. 15 min), sender name, cadence/touchpoint count, and email/SMS sequencing.
  3. Evaluate permanently removing the fifth email given equal-or-better performance with fewer sends.
  4. Keep optimizing click-to-conversion rather than open rate, since post-open engagement is the main driver of the lift.
  5. Apply the same creative/messaging framework to other upper-funnel abandonment journeys to see if it generalizes.

Additional notes

  • This test's control (Cohort A) is itself the 10-day cadence that won the earlier Cadence & Timing test — this Super Test redesigns creative, sender, trigger timing, and cadence on top of that win. Full progression on Eligibility Check CVR: 2.93% (original 21-day series) → 3.07% (10-day cadence) → 4.49% (this redesigned series) — each step compounding on the last.
