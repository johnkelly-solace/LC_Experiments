Impact summary

Inconclusive results across both send-times and contexts. The A/B test setup limited the ability to isolate results and is a meaningful contributor to why this was mixed/inconclusive rather than a clean win or loss. Reverting to baseline content (A) and reconfiguring the testing structure (event-based instead of A/B) to get cleaner reads going forward.

Learnings

What worked

  • Prominent reschedule CTA (button vs. inline text) likely drove higher reschedule rates in both flows for B — not necessarily bad, since cancellations were also lower.
  • 1-Day flow: CVR held flat/slightly improved despite a big click drop, suggesting the content itself (not just clicks) may have driven the effect.
  • Re-ran the analysis on human-filtered opens/clicks specifically to rule out bot contamination — confirmed the engagement gaps were real human behavior, not noise.

What didn't

  • 3-Hour flow: educational content correlated with higher no-show and lower attended-call rates — the opposite direction from the 1-Day flow.
  • Non-clickable content blocks plus the A/B (not event-based) test structure left the team unable to directly attribute the 1-Day flow's CVR to the content itself.
  • Unequal clickable surface area between variants (A had 2 click targets, B had 1) made CTR/CTO comparisons apples-to-oranges.
  • An open-rate gap between variants, despite identical subject line/preview text, was never conclusively resolved — possibly a rendering/tracking-pixel artifact.

Follow-up actions

  1. Revert to baseline content (A) rather than ship B.
  2. Move appointment-reminder testing to an event-based format instead of the CIO A/B format, to get cleaner reads.
  3. Balance clickable content evenly across future variants to isolate content/design effects from raw click-opportunity differences.

Additional notes

  • Testing in appointment reminders will be primarily event-based moving forward, replacing the A/B CIO format used here.
